**Goal:** feel the difference context makes, on one real task. **Time:** 20 minutes.

> [!tip] Nothing ready to work on?
> Use [[Practice - Meeting Notes to Actions]]. It includes fictional input and an answer checklist, so you can complete this lesson without preparing your own material.

## Steps

1. **Write a tiny CLAUDE.md.** In your vault, create a note named exactly `CLAUDE.md` with four or five lines: your specialty, who you write for, and how you like things written.

    ```markdown
    I am an ENT surgeon. I treat adults.
    Patients: about 8th grade reading level, warm and direct, short sentences.
    Handouts: what it is, why it matters, what to expect, when to call.
    ```

2. **Ask for one real piece of work** that needs no patient data. Pick one:
    - A one-page patient handout on a common procedure.
    - A thank-you letter to a referring doctor (use `[Patient]` as a placeholder).
    - A summary of a paper you drop into the folder, against a question you care about.
    - A weekly plan from a messy brain dump.
3. **Refine it twice.** Tell Claude what to fix in plain words.
4. **Save the recipe** so next time takes seconds.

## Do it with Claude

```text
Read CLAUDE.md. Using that voice, write a one-page patient handout on what
to expect after [procedure]. Save it in this folder. Then list any clinical
statements I should double-check.
```

After you are happy with it:

```text
Save the steps we just used as a reusable recipe in skills/patient-handout.md
so I can run it for any topic.
```

## Checkpoint

- [ ] A finished piece of work in your vault.
- [ ] Your first recipe in `skills/`.
- [ ] You checked every clinical statement yourself.
