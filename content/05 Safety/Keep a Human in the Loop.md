AI can draft, sort, summarize, and build. It should not be the one deciding things that need your judgment, and it should not take irreversible actions you have not seen. The pattern is the same one you use with a new resident: they do the work, you review before it counts.

## The default: draft, review, approve

For anything that leaves your desk:

1. The AI **drafts** (the email, the letter, the handout, the plan).
2. You **review**, and edit or send it back.
3. Then it **finishes**, or you do.

Write this into your `CLAUDE.md`: "Never send email, post, or share anything without my explicit approval."

## What always needs you

- Anything that touches **clinical decisions**: diagnosis, treatment, dosing, triage of patients. AI can help you think and can summarize evidence; the decision is yours.
- Anything that goes to a **patient**.
- Anything with your **name on it**: letters, manuscripts, reviews, recommendations.
- Anything **irreversible**: sending, deleting, submitting, paying, publishing.

## What can earn more independence

Low-stakes, reversible, internal tasks you have watched work many times:

- Filing notes into folders (it moves, never deletes).
- Writing your morning brief or weekly digest.
- Tidying formatting, fixing broken links.
- Drafting replies into your drafts folder.

Even for these, check results periodically. See the "silent drift" section of [[C4 - Cadence]].

## Approvals in Claude

Claude asks before taking significant actions. Keep those prompts on at first. When a specific recipe has proven reliable, you can let it run with fewer interruptions, but scope that trust narrowly to that recipe, not to everything.

## You are accountable

Anything an AI does on your behalf is, professionally and legally, something you did. That is not a reason to avoid it; it is a reason to set it up so you always know what it is doing.
