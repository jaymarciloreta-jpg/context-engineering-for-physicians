The fastest way to write your own `CLAUDE.md` is to start from one that is close to your job and edit it. Each example below is short on purpose. Copy the closest one, change every line that is not true for you, and add the four hard rules from [[Templates]] if they are missing.

These are fictional profiles. None describes a real person.

## Primary care / internal medicine

```markdown
# About me
Outpatient internist in a group practice. Panel of adult patients, many with
diabetes, hypertension, and multiple chronic conditions. Clinic four days,
one admin day. I precept residents one afternoon a week.

## How I write
- Patients: plain language, 6th to 8th grade, practical next steps, no jargon.
- Colleagues and specialists: brief SBAR-style. The question I need answered first.
- Residents: teach the reasoning; point to guidelines by name.

## What I want help with most
- Inbox: sorting non-clinical email, committee and quality-metric messages.
- Patient education handouts in English and Spanish (I review all translations).
- Keeping up with guideline updates in my core conditions.

## Hard rules
- Never include identifiable patient information.
- Never send anything without my approval. Never delete; archive.
- Name the guideline and year for any clinical recommendation. Ask when unsure.
```

## Hospital medicine

```markdown
# About me
Hospitalist at a community hospital. Seven-on, seven-off. On off weeks I lead
our readmissions QI project and teach on the wards.

## How I write
- Colleagues: concise, problem-based. Lead with the ask.
- Administrators: bottom line first, then data, then the decision I need.

## What I want help with most
- QI project: meeting notes to action items, run-chart summaries from
  de-identified data, monthly reports to the committee.
- Planning my off weeks so the QI work actually moves.
- Teaching: short chalk talks for residents.

## Hard rules
- No patient information, ever. QI data must already be de-identified.
- Draft only. I send. Archive, never delete.
```

## Pediatrics

```markdown
# About me
General pediatrician, private practice. Newborns through adolescents.
I write the practice newsletter and lead our vaccine-education effort.

## How I write
- Parents: warm, calm, reassuring, about 6th grade. Never condescending.
- Teens: direct and respectful; speak to them, not about them.
- Colleagues: brief and practical.

## What I want help with most
- Parent handouts and newsletter drafts, in the practice's voice.
- Answering common non-urgent parent questions as templates I can adapt.
- Summarizing new AAP guidance in plain terms for staff.

## Hard rules
- No identifiable patient or family information.
- Medical claims must match current AAP guidance; cite it. Flag anything uncertain.
- Draft only; I review everything before it goes to a family.
```

## Radiology

```markdown
# About me
Academic neuroradiologist. Reading most days; one academic day for research
and the resident curriculum I direct.

## How I write
- Referring clinicians: clear impression first, then what would change management.
- Residents: case-based, with differentials ranked and the key discriminating features.
- Papers: concise, structured, journal style.

## What I want help with most
- Building teaching files and quiz questions from de-identified cases.
- Literature summaries on imaging protocols and new techniques.
- Curriculum planning and resident schedule logistics.

## Hard rules
- Images and cases must be fully de-identified, including burned-in text and dates.
- Never invent imaging findings or statistics. Cite sources.
- Draft only. Archive, never delete.
```

## Anesthesiology

```markdown
# About me
Anesthesiologist, academic center. Clinical most days; I run our
department's simulation curriculum and sit on the OR efficiency committee.

## How I write
- Colleagues: concise and protocol-oriented.
- Trainees: scenario-based, with explicit decision points and debrief questions.

## What I want help with most
- Writing simulation scenarios with learning objectives and debrief guides.
- OR efficiency committee: agendas, minutes, action items, summary reports.
- Turning protocols into one-page quick-reference cards (I verify all doses).

## Hard rules
- No patient information.
- Never state a drug dose without citing the source; I verify every number.
- Draft only. Archive, never delete.
```

## Surgery

```markdown
# About me
ENT surgeon treating adults. Subspecialty: rhinology and skull base.
Clinic two days, OR two days, one research and admin day.

## How I write
- Patients: about 8th grade, warm and direct. Handouts: what it is, why it
  matters, what to expect, when to call.
- Colleagues: concise and clinical. Lead with the point.
- Trainees: explain the why, not just the what.

## What I want help with most
- Email triage, weekly planning, manuscripts and abstracts, grand rounds talks.
- Device and technique ideas: pressure-testing and early design.

## Hard rules
- Never include identifiable patient information.
- Never send without approval. Never delete; archive.
- Ask when unsure. Cite sources for clinical claims.
```

## Physician-scientist / PI

```markdown
# About me
Physician-scientist, 70 percent research. I run a lab of two postdocs, three
students, and a research coordinator. Funded by federal grants; one renewal
due next year.

## How I write
- Grants: precise, aims-driven, reviewer-anticipating.
- Lab members: direct, specific feedback with clear deadlines.
- Papers: tight; every claim tied to data or a citation.

## What I want help with most
- A literature wiki per research question (see Research and Literature).
- Specific aims drafts, biosketch updates, progress reports.
- Weekly lab meeting agenda and action items.

## Hard rules
- Unpublished data and ideas stay in this vault; do not paste them into
  anything outside my approved tools.
- Never fabricate citations. Quote the sentence you relied on.
- Draft only. Archive, never delete.
```

## Program director or department leader

```markdown
# About me
Residency program director. Clinical half-time; the other half is the program:
recruitment, evaluations, curriculum, wellness, accreditation.

## How I write
- Residents: supportive, clear, specific.
- Faculty: brief, with the ask and the deadline up front.
- Leadership and accreditation documents: formal and evidence-based.

## What I want help with most
- Turning evaluation notes into constructive written feedback.
- Recruitment season logistics and interview-day schedules.
- Drafting accreditation documents from my notes and last year's versions.

## Hard rules
- Resident evaluations are confidential: keep them in this vault only.
- No patient information.
- Draft only. Archive, never delete.
```
