Plain-language definitions of every technical term on this site.

**Agent.** An AI that can take steps on its own to finish a task (read a file, search, write, check, repeat), instead of just answering one question.

**Approval (or permission prompt).** When Claude stops and asks before taking an action like sending, editing, or connecting. Keep these on until you trust a task.

**Archive.** The folder for finished work. Moving things there instead of deleting keeps your history searchable.

**BAA (Business Associate Agreement).** A legal agreement under HIPAA that a vendor signs with a healthcare organization before handling patient data. Your institution decides which tools are covered. See [[Patient Privacy and PHI]].

**CLAUDE.md.** A plain text file that Claude reads automatically when it opens a folder. Your "about me" file at the top of the vault, and short project briefs inside project folders. See [[C1 - Context]].

**Connector.** A safe, sign-in-based link that lets the AI read from or act in another tool, such as your calendar or email. See [[C2 - Connections]].

**Context.** Everything the AI knows about you and the task before it starts. The more relevant and concise, the better the result.

**Context engineering.** Setting up the AI once with the right background, connections, and recipes, instead of re-explaining yourself in every prompt.

**Context rot.** The tendency of AI to recall details less reliably as a conversation gets very long or stuffed with information. The fix: one task per conversation and lean context files.

**Cowork.** The name Anthropic's help pages use for Claude working on tasks in your folders and tools from the desktop app.

**De-identified.** Data with every identifier removed so no one could figure out who the patient is. See [[Patient Privacy and PHI]].

**Frontmatter (properties).** The block between `---` lines at the top of a note, holding details like `status:` and `tags:`.

**Hallucination.** When AI states something false with confidence, such as a citation that does not exist. See [[When AI Gets It Wrong]].

**Markdown.** A simple way of formatting plain text: `#` for headings, `-` for bullets, `**bold**` for bold. Obsidian notes are markdown files ending in `.md`.

**MCP (Model Context Protocol).** The open standard that connectors are built on. You never need to deal with it directly.

**PARA.** A folder layout: Projects, Areas, Resources, Archive. Created by productivity writer Tiago Forte. See [[C1 - Context]].

**PHI (Protected Health Information).** Health information that can be tied to an individual patient.

**Progressive disclosure.** Giving the AI a short summary first and letting it open details only when needed, so it stays focused.

**Prompt.** What you type to the AI. In this approach, prompts get shorter because the context does the heavy lifting.

**Prompt injection.** Text hidden inside an email, document, or web page that tries to give the AI instructions. A reason to keep approvals on for anything that sends or shares.

**Quartz.** The free tool that turns a folder of Obsidian notes into a website, like this one.

**Recipe (skill).** A saved, plain-language checklist for a task you repeat. See [[C3 - Capabilities]] and the [[Recipe Library]].

**Scheduled task.** A recipe set to run automatically at a set time. See [[C4 - Cadence]].

**Vault.** Obsidian's name for your notes folder.

**Wikilink.** A link between notes written as `[[Note name]]`.
