You do not need much to get started. Here are the four tools that matter, what each one is in plain terms, why it helps, and the order to set them up. **You can stop after the first two and still get most of the value.**

Think of it like an operating room. Obsidian is the back table, where everything is laid out and labeled. Claude is the assistant who actually does the work. GitHub is the archive where finished work is stored and shared. Antigravity is a power tool for when you want to build something custom.

| Tool | Role | Cost | Needed? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Obsidian | Your knowledge base | Free | Yes |
| Claude (desktop app) | Your AI assistant | Paid plan | Yes |
| GitHub | Backup, history, publishing | Free | Optional |
| Antigravity | Build custom apps and sites | Free tier | Optional |

## 1. Obsidian: your knowledge base

**What it is:** a free app for writing and organizing notes. Your notes are plain text files (called markdown, ending in `.md`) that live on your own computer, so they are yours, they are private, and they will still open in 20 years.

**Why it matters:** this is where your "second brain" lives. Templates, references, project pages, meeting notes, and your "about me" file all sit here in one searchable place. When you set the AI up, this is the knowledge it reads. Good notes here are what make every answer specific to you instead of generic.

**Set it up first:** [[Install Obsidian]].

## 2. Claude: your AI assistant

**What it is:** the Claude desktop app from Anthropic. Beyond chat, it can work directly in a folder on your computer: read your notes, draft documents, build slides and spreadsheets, and connect to your other tools. Anthropic's help pages call this way of working **Cowork**.

**Why it matters:** this is the part that does the work. Point it at your Obsidian folder and it can use everything you have written. Connect it to your email, calendar, or reference library (these links are called connectors) and it can act for you, not just advise. It can also save step-by-step recipes, called **skills**, and run tasks on a schedule.

**Cost:** requires a paid Claude plan (Pro, Max, Team, or Enterprise). Check with your institution first; some offer an approved enterprise plan.

**Set it up second:** [[Set Up Claude]]. Obsidian plus Claude is the core, and most people get most of the benefit right here.

## 3. GitHub: backup, history, and publishing (optional)

**What it is:** a free service for storing files online, keeping a history of every change, and publishing simple websites.

**Why it matters:** if you ever want to share your notes as a website (this wiki is published that way) or keep a full version history of your knowledge base, GitHub is the most common free way to do it. Claude can handle almost all of the steps for you.

**Set it up when you are ready to publish.** Many people never need it.

## 4. Antigravity: the power tool (optional)

**What it is:** a free app from Google where AI agents do real building work. You describe what you want, and the agent plans it, builds it, tests it, and can deploy it. It is built for making software and websites.

**Why it matters:** when you want a custom tool, such as a small calculator for your clinic, a research dashboard, or a polished custom website, this is one way to build it without writing the code yourself. It is more advanced than the others, so treat it as optional.

**Cost:** a free individual tier with a weekly usage limit, with paid upgrades through Google's AI plans (as of late 2026).

## The short version

- Start with **Obsidian** (your knowledge) and **Claude** (your assistant). That is the core.
- Add **GitHub** if you want to back up with full history or publish.
- Add **Antigravity** only if you want to build custom tools.

## A note on choices

These are recommendations, not the only options. Other note apps and other AI assistants exist, and the method on this site (plain notes, a context file, connectors, recipes, schedules) carries over to most of them. If your hospital or practice has its own approved tools, use those for anything involving patient information. See [[Patient Privacy and PHI]].
